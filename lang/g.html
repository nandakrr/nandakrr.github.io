🆓 Free resources

  • DW Learn German (dw.com): the free course "Nicos Weg" takes you from A1 to B1 with videos and exercises. It's the best free course available, so do it alongside this app.
  • Easy German (YouTube): real Germans on the street, with German and English subtitles.
  • Goethe-Institut: free sample A1/A2/B1 exams with audio.
  • Tandem / HelloTalk: free language exchange. Germans who want to learn English will happily practise German with you.
  • Once you're in Germany: the VHS (Volkshochschule) offers cheap courses, and the government-funded Integrationskurs may be free or subsidised for you.

How to actually learn German

1. Learn nouns with der / die / das, always

Not "Tisch" but "der Tisch". Helpful patterns: -ung, -heit, -keit, -ion, -tät → die. -chen, -lein, -ment, -um → das. Male people, days, months and seasons → der.

2. The verb is always in position 2

"Heute gehe ich ins Kino." Not "Heute ich gehe". In questions without a W-word the verb comes first: "Hast du Zeit?" After weil / dass / wenn, the verb goes to the end: "..., weil ich Deutsch lerne."

3. Pronunciation that helps you a lot

  • w = English "v" (Wasser = "Vasser"). v = "f" (Vater = "Fater"). z = "ts" (Zeit = "Tsait").
  • ei = "eye" (mein, Zeit). ie = "ee" (Bier, viel). This is easy to mix up!
  • sp / st at the start = "shp / sht" (Sport, Straße).
  • ä ≈ "e" in "bed". ö: say "e" with round lips. ü: say "ee" with round lips.
  • ch after a/o/u = throat sound (Buch). After e/i = soft hiss (ich, nicht).

4. Speak from day one

Don't wait until your grammar is perfect. Germans appreciate any effort, and if they switch to English, politely say: "Ich möchte Deutsch üben, bitte." (I'd like to practise German, please.)

5. Daily German input

Switch your phone to German, listen to Easy German or the DW news in slow German (Langsam gesprochene Nachrichten), and label things at home with sticky notes.
