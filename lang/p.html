ten, ta or to? (the gender game)

Every Polish noun is masculine (ten), feminine (ta) or neuter (to). Adjectives and past-tense verbs change with it, so it matters. Usually the ending tells you, and this game trains that instinct.

How to actually learn Polish

1. Don't panic about the cases

Polish nouns change their endings (7 "cases"). Don't learn tables at the start. Learn whole phrases: "do pracy" (to work), "w pracy" (at work), "z kolegą" (with a colleague). The patterns will come.

2. The ending tells you the gender

Consonant → ten (masculine) · -a → ta (feminine) · -o, -e, -ę → to (neuter). Exceptions are mostly male people: mężczyzna (man), kolega (colleague).

3. Pronunciation that helps you a lot

  • w = v · ł = w · c = ts · j = y
  • sz = sh · cz = ch · rz / ż = the "s" in measure · ch = throaty h
  • ś ć ź ń (or si, ci, zi, ni) = softer versions: say them with your tongue pressed up behind your teeth
  • ą = nasal "on", ę = nasal "en", ó = u, y = the "i" in bit
  • Stress: almost always the second-to-last syllable.

4. Verbs come in pairs

robić / zrobić (to do / to get it done), kupować / kupić (to buy / to buy once). The second one is used for finished actions and the future. Learn them together.

5. Pan / Pani = formal "you"

With strangers, officials and older people, use pan (man) / pani (woman) with the "he/she" verb form: "Czy może pan mi pomóc?" Using "ty" too early can sound rude.

6. Speak from day one

Poles really appreciate foreigners speaking Polish, even badly. If they switch to English, say: "Uczę się polskiego, proszę mówić po polsku." (I'm learning Polish, please speak Polish.)
